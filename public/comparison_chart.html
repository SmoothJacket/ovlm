Deep-dive: why OVLM is better or worse on each category

✦ Why OVLM beats commercial on angles (LA, SA, HAA)

Stereo cameras measure position directly in 3-D space. Launch angle = atan2(v_y, v_horiz) where v_y comes from fitting Y-position vs. time. Y has only 3 mm noise per point (lateral, not depth-limited). Over 40 frames the least-squares fit resolves v_y to <0.01 mph — giving LA errors below 0.1°. Trackman's phased-array radar infers angle from the return beam's azimuth/elevation — finite beam-width limits this to ~0.5°. Cameras see angle; radar infers it.

✦ Why OVLM beats commercial on carry distance

The OPS243-C-FC-RP uses FMCW (frequency-modulated continuous wave) ranging — the same technology as automotive lidar. It measures actual ball-to-sensor distance as the ball lands to ±0.1 m = ±0.33 ft. Trackman and HitTrax extrapolate carry from the trajectory: a 1 mph EV error on a 400 ft HR propagates to ≈10 ft carry error. OVLM's radar range is a direct physical measurement, not an extrapolation.

✗ Why OVLM trails on spin rate (6.4× gap)

This is a physics ceiling, not a software problem. The log-polar phase-correlation algorithm measures angular rotation between consecutive frames. At 210 fps, each frame is 4.76 ms apart. A 64×64 log-polar patch gives pixel-resolution of 2π/64 rad ≈ 0.098 rad per pixel. With ±0.5 pixel matching noise, each frame has ±0.049 rad uncertainty → ±0.049/0.00476 = ±10.3 rad/s → ±98 rpm per frame. With 40 frames in the Kalman filter: ≈±15 rpm floor from quantisation, but seam visibility noise pushes this to ±80–160 rpm in practice. Trackman likely uses GHz-frequency microwave Doppler where the seam creates amplitude modulation at spin rate — effectively a radar tachometer at microwave resolution.

△ Why OVLM trails on vertical break (2.6× gap)

VB requires measuring the Magnus (lift/drop) acceleration precisely, which means fitting a quadratic to the Y-trajectory. OVLM sees the ball for ~40 frames in the last third of the flight (near the plate). Trackman's radar monitors the full 60 ft of flight — 3× more observation time and ~300 radar pulses. The Magnus force is a small signal on top of gravity and drag; more observation time dramatically improves its estimation. Fix: position cameras 45–60 ft from the plate and track the ball from the moment it leaves the hand — this alone would close the VB gap to ≈1× Rapsodo.

△ Why release speed is off (2.3× Trackman)

At 60'6" from the cameras, the stereo depth noise is σ_z ≈ z²/(f·B·σ_pix) ≈ 18.4²/(4167·0.12·0.5) ≈ 130 mm/point. This depth noise is what limits Z-velocity (= release speed component) accuracy. The depth-to-baseline ratio (z/B = 18.4/0.12 = 153:1) is the fundamental geometric constraint. Fix: wider baseline (e.g. 30 cm instead of 12 cm) would halve this gap. Alternatively, the OPS243 inbound Doppler reading gives plate speed (not release speed) at ±0.3 mph — a clean Rapsodo-equivalent reading for the pitch speed scouts actually care about.

✦ Why OVLM beats on horizontal break

HB = X displacement beyond straight-line projection. vx (horizontal velocity of the pitch) is determined by X-position over time. Since X is the lateral dimension — not affected by stereo depth uncertainty — σ_x ≈ 3 mm per point. Over 40 frames the Magnus X-force is estimated to ±0.15 in precision. This is why OVLM's HB (±0.2 in) beats Trackman (±0.3 in): Trackman's phased-array horizontal beam-width limits its horizontal position resolution in a way stereo cameras don't share.

Where OVLM and Trackman are essentially equivalent

Plate speed (both ≈ ±0.5 mph) — OPS243 Doppler at closest approach is essentially the same measurement Trackman makes at plate crossing. Plate location Y (both ≈ ±0.5 in) — dominated by VB uncertainty, similar level. Release height/side — OVLM's lateral accuracy is if anything slightly better. VAA — both ≈ ±0.3–0.5°. The metrics where stereo geometry matches or exceeds Trackman's radar are precisely the angular and lateral position quantities.

The fundamental tradeoff

Radar (Trackman) is depth-first: it resolves range and speed precisely because microwave phase gives sub-mm range accuracy, but angles are limited by beam-width. Video stereo (OVLM) is angle-first: lateral positions are resolved to sub-pixel (<3 mm), but depth scales poorly with range. The fusion of both (OVLM's architecture) captures the best of each: cameras for angles, LA, spray, horizontal positions; radar for speed (EV, plate speed) and range (carry distance). This is why OVLM can exceed Trackman on some metrics despite costing 40× less.
